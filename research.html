Research

Forecasting Under Low Signal-to-Noise

Jan 2026 – present

Machine Learning Research Intern · McGill Networks Research Lab · Supervisor: Prof. Mark Coates

I study how deep learning and foundation models forecast time series with a low signal-to-noise ratio, with financial data as one example. I evaluated SLiDE, a Koopman recurrent model, against standard baselines and foundation models such as Chronos and TabPFN, and I am now building a TabPFN–SLiDE hybrid that holds up under noise.

Bayesian Spatiotemporal Forecasting

Oct 2026

Machine Learning Research Intern · McGill Networks Research Lab · Supervisor: Prof. Mark Coates

Many forecasting problems involve variables linked by a network, such as sensors along a road network. The goal of this project is a Bayesian version of SLiDE that reports how uncertain its forecasts are and uses the graph that connects its inputs.

I worked on adding Bayesian graph priors to this Bayesian graphical formulation of SLiDE. The work led to a co-authored paper accepted at the Asilomar Conference on Signals, Systems, and Computers 2026.

Human–Robot Interaction for Elderly Care

Sep – Dec 2025

Robotics Research Assistant · McGill NeuRoC Lab · Supervisor: Prof. Guillaume Durandau

Human–robot interaction can help older adults live more independently. A common task is co-manipulating a heavy object, usually with admittance control. Three gaps remain:

  • Most research relies on physical experiments, which limits scale and accessibility.
  • Simulators treat the human as an input rather than a model, so the coupled human–robot dynamics go unstudied.
  • Few works address tremor, which matters in elderly care.

To address these, I built a MuJoCo simulation that couples a musculoskeletal human arm with a robot arm, and implemented an admittance–impedance controller with a Butterworth filter to suppress tremor.

The results are in my first-author extended abstract accepted at ICNR 2026 (poster). The code is at NeuRoC-Lab/HRI-controller, and there is a short write-up on LinkedIn.
